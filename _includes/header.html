Master of Science in Language & Communication Technologies

I am an MSc graduate working at the intersection of mechanistic interpretability, AI alignment, and fairness in LLMs. My research centers on a question I find both technically fascinating and consequential: when LLMs encode biases, such as stereotypes around gender, age, sexual orientation, and physical appearance, are those representations universal across languages, or do they vary in ways that reflect underlying cultural and linguistic structures? During my master's studies, I was introduced to mechanistic interpretability through a seminar and software project taught by DFKI researchers. That exposure led me to propose a thesis grounded in this framework, which my supervisors Josef, Koel, and David supported. The work was submitted to the MRL workshop at EMNLP 2025, where it received the Best Paper Award. Our follow-up investigation into cross-lingual stereotype bias across multiple model families and architectures is currently under review at EMNLP ARR 2026.
